# SALSA 2.0 — Lightweight Neural Cryptanalysis of LWE/RLWE

A CPU-first, from-scratch reimplementation and extension of the SALSA attack on
Learning With Errors, built to test one question: **how small can the model be
and still recover a sparse binary secret?**

The current model, **NACT-F**, has **4,238,208 trainable parameters** and
performs the complete attack — learn, recover, verify — at n=12 and n=20 on a
CPU, in about 20 minutes of training per dimension.

> **This is not a practical break of LWE or RLWE.** The demonstrated instances
> are diagnostic: n=12 with h=2 has C(12,2) = 66 possible secrets and n=20 with
> h=2 has C(20,2) = 190. See [Limitations](#limitations).

---

## Project overview

**SALSA** (Wenger, Chen, Charton, Lauter, NeurIPS 2022) attacks LWE by training
a sequence-to-sequence transformer on public samples `(A, b)` to predict `b`
from `A`. If the model learns the map well enough, it has implicitly learned the
secret, and the secret can be read back out by feeding the model chosen inputs
it never saw in training.

**The cryptanalytic objective** is therefore not prediction accuracy. It is
recovering `s` exactly, from public data only, and then confirming that
recovered `s` independently.

**SALSA 2.0** changes three things:

1. **Scale.** The original uses roughly 51M parameters. This project caps the
   budget at 4–5M and asks whether the attack survives — a ~12× reduction.
2. **Input representation.** The original spends `width` digit tokens per
   coordinate. SALSA 2.0 spends **one token per coordinate**, which halves the
   encoder sequence and makes absolute coordinate identity expressible.
3. **Methodology.** Every stage is separated so the attack cannot accidentally
   consult the answer, and every recovered secret is independently verified
   against fresh samples.

**NACT-F** is the final model: the Numerical-Aware Compact Transformer reduced,
by ablation, to just the one-token representation. It is the smallest variant
tested that still completes the whole pipeline.

**Why lightweight and reproducible.** Every experiment here runs on a laptop
CPU with no CUDA. That constraint is the point: it makes each result cheap to
re-run, and it forces the architecture to earn its capacity rather than buy
results with parameters.

## Mathematical formulation

The Learning With Errors problem. Given many samples

```
b = A s + e   (mod q)
```

recover the secret `s` from `(A, b)` alone.

| symbol | meaning | value here |
|---|---|---|
| `q` | modulus, a prime | 251 |
| `n` | lattice dimension — length of the secret | 12, 20 |
| `A` | public matrix, rows in `Z_q^n` | RLWE circulant rows |
| `s` | **the secret**, a binary vector in `{0,1}^n` | unknown to the attack |
| `h` | Hamming weight of `s` — **the number of non-zero secret coordinates** | 2 |
| `e` | error/noise vector, small, drawn per sample | discrete Gaussian |
| `sigma` | standard deviation of the error distribution | 3 |
| `b` | public target, one integer in `Z_q` per row | computed as above |

The secret is **sparse**: exactly `h` of the `n` coordinates are 1. That
sparsity is what makes the search space small enough to be diagnostic, and it is
also a trap — an all-zeros guess already scores `(n-h)/n` coordinate accuracy
for free (0.833 at n=12, 0.900 at n=20), so **coordinate accuracy alone is never
evidence of recovery**.

In the **RLWE** variant used here, rows of `A` are not independent: each block of
`n` rows is generated by rotating a single vector, which is what deployed
ring-based schemes do and what the SALSA paper studies.

## Original SALSA

Established by direct inspection of the released source during this project's
fidelity audit (`results/original_fidelity_audit/`):

- **Representation.** Integers are written as base-81 digits, least-significant
  first, fixed width, with no separator — `2n + 2` tokens for an `n`-dimensional
  row. SALSA 2.0's encoder reproduces this token-for-token; that equivalence was
  verified by executing the original `encoders.py` against ours.
- **Model.** A Universal Transformer: one shared layer applied repeatedly, with
  a learned copy gate. The paper credits sharing plus gating with a large
  sample-efficiency gain.
- **Direct recovery.** Feed the model the chosen input `a = K·e_i`. Since
  `a·s = K·s_i mod q`, the answer should be near `0` when `s_i = 0` and near `K`
  when `s_i = 1`. Reading all `n` coordinates reconstructs a candidate secret.

**Limitations found in this project's audit**, all tied to evidence in
`results/original_fidelity_audit/`:

- The released `evaluator.py` resolves candidate polarity **against the true
  secret** and reports the best of six variants scored that way. That cannot be
  part of an attack, so SALSA 2.0 replaced it with an anchored rule that needs no
  ground truth.
- The paper's residual verification step (§4.4) is **not implemented** in the
  released code. SALSA 2.0 implements it.
- The supplied `transformer.py` had been modernised to **disable** the Universal
  Transformer looping and the copy gate — the two features the paper credits
  most. SALSA 2.0 keeps them, so it is architecturally closer to the paper than
  that fork.
- **Scaling concerns.** The original uses roughly 51M parameters and ~3.9M
  distinct training samples, with each reused about ten times. Its `K` values are
  reduced modulo `B^int_len` rather than modulo `q`, which for several published
  values leaves the probe outside `Z_q` entirely.

## SALSA 2.0 architecture

### Representation: the key change

| | original / V1 | NACT / NACT-F |
|---|---|---|
| tokens per coordinate | `width` = 2 digit tokens | **1 coordinate token** |
| encoder sequence length | `2n + 2` | **`n + 2`** |
| at n=12 | 26 positions | **14** |
| at n=128 | 258 positions | **130** |
| coordinate identity | RoPE only (relative) | learned **absolute** coordinate embedding |

Why absolute identity matters: `b = Σ aᵢ sᵢ` binds coordinate `i` to secret bit
`sᵢ`, so the model needs to know *which* coordinate it is looking at. Rotary
embeddings encode only relative offsets. With one token per coordinate, position
*is* coordinate index, and an absolute embedding becomes expressible.

The coordinate table is a fixed `128 × 512` matrix, so **the parameter count does
not change with `n`** — one architecture covers n=12 through n=128.

### Full NACT vs NACT-F

**Full NACT** (4,241,288 parameters) adds, on top of the coordinate token:
a centered residue feature, a `cos`/`sin` Fourier pair (the characters of `Z_q`),
a zero/non-zero indicator, a learned zero-coordinate vector, and a per-head
attention bias on zero coordinates.

**NACT-F** (4,238,208 parameters) is full NACT with **all six of those removed**,
keeping only the one-token representation, the base-81 digit embeddings it is
built from, and the absolute coordinate embedding.

The ablation found NACT-F matched full NACT within one-seed noise on every
primary learning metric, and completed secret recovery just as well. **That is
why the one-token representation — not the numerical features — is the important
simplification.** NACT-F is the model this repository ships.

### Model dimensions

| | encoder | decoder |
|---|---|---|
| width | 512 | 128 |
| heads | 8 | 4 |
| parameter sets | 1 (shared) | 1 (shared) |
| passes | `T_e = 2` | `T_d = 2` |
| FFN | multiplier 4 (hidden 2048) | hidden 512 |

RMSNorm, GELU, no biases, RoPE on self-attention, copy gate on every layer.
Vocabulary 85. Loops reuse the shared parameter set, so effective depth costs no
parameters.

**CPU-oriented design.** Pure PyTorch, no CUDA dependency, no custom kernels, no
sequential scans — embeddings, dense matmuls and softmax only. Every result in
this repository was produced on a laptop CPU.

## Experimental results

**Two different things are measured and they must not be confused.**

### Prediction metrics — how well the model predicts `b`

| n | h | model | parameters | samples | validation loss | acc_tau | exact integer accuracy | token accuracy |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 2 | NACT-F | 4,238,208 | 100,032 | 0.9891 | 0.9839 | 0.0913 | 0.6934 |
| 20 | 2 | NACT-F | 4,238,208 | 100,032 | 0.9764 | 0.9863 | 0.1035 | 0.6995 |
| 12 | 2 | V1 GatedUT | 4,131,200 | 100,032 | 1.7413 | 0.3550 | 0.0078 | 0.5324 |
| 20 | 2 | V1 GatedUT | 4,131,200 | 200,000 | 1.8233 | 0.2344 | 0.0034 | 0.4590 |

Baselines, computed exactly from the representation: chance `acc_tau` **0.19287**,
chance exact integer accuracy **0.00398**, chance token accuracy **0.44622**. A
model that learned only the output marginals scores **1.86498** nats — that, not
the uniform 4.44265, is the real zero point for loss.

`acc_tau` is SALSA's metric: the fraction of predictions with `|b − b̂| ≤ 0.1·q`.
**Exact integer accuracy** is the fraction where the predicted integer `b̂`
equals `b` exactly. Neither is secret recovery.

### Secret recovery metrics — did the attack recover `s`?

| n | h | exact secret recovery | coordinate accuracy | Hamming distance | successful K | residual verification |
|---:|---:|:---:|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| 12 | 2 | **YES** | 1.0000 | 0 | 8/10 | **PASS** |
| 20 | 2 | **YES** | 1.0000 | 0 | 8/10 | **PASS** |
| 12 | 2 (V1) | NO | 0.1667 | 10 | 0/10 | — |

The two K values that do not succeed are `K = 1` and `K = 250`, the deliberate
**negative controls**: their ring separation is 1, so the two hypotheses are
nearly the same target and they *should* fail.

Verification residual standard deviation: **2.998** at n=12 and **2.941** at
n=20, against a configured `sigma` of 3.0 — while every incorrect candidate sits
near 72.5, the uniform-over-`Z_q` reference. That ~24× separation is the evidence.

**Exact integer accuracy of 0.09 and exact secret recovery are different claims.**
The model gets the exact integer right about a tenth of the time, and recovers
the secret completely. Recovery does not need per-sample perfection; it needs the
probe responses to fall on the correct side of the anchor.

## End-to-end pipeline

```text
public LWE/RLWE data
        ↓
     encoding                LatticeCodec: base 81, lsb-first, no separator
        ↓
      NACT-F                 one coordinate token per aᵢ, encoder length n+2
        ↓
     predict b               greedy decode
        ↓
direct secret recovery       STAGE 1
        ↓
   candidate secret
        ↓
independent residual
    verification             STAGE 2
        ↓
     evaluation              STAGE 3
```

### Ground-truth isolation

The stages are separated because collapsing them is how a recovery experiment
fools itself:

| stage | what it is allowed to see |
|---|---|
| **recovery** | public probes and model predictions only. `DirectRecovery` has no parameter through which a secret could be passed — a test asserts this against the source. |
| **verification** | `(A, b, candidate, q, sigma)` and nothing else. No secret, no model output, no training label. |
| **evaluation** | the only stage that loads the true secret, and it runs **last** — after a candidate *and* a verification verdict already exist. |

Running with `evaluate=False` performs the whole attack with no ground truth
available at all, which is the real adversarial setting.

### Recovery rule

Two secret-free rules ship. `separation` means the ring distance between the two
hypotheses, `min(K mod q, q − K mod q)` — a function of `K` and `q` only.

- **`aggregate`** (default) — separation-weighted vote across the whole K sweep.
  An uninformative probe contributes almost nothing.
- **`best_margin`** (diagnostic) — the single highest-margin K, guarded by
  `min_separation`, which defaults to `floor(sigma) + 1` (= 4 at sigma = 3).

The guard exists because the unguarded rule is degenerate: at `K = 1` the two
anchors are adjacent, so a model answering a constant scores the *maximum*
possible margin. Without the guard it selected a negative control at n=20.
Excluded K are still measured and still vote with their small weight — they are
excluded from *selection*, not from the evidence.

## Reproducibility

Run the complete pipeline against an existing checkpoint. **Neither command
trains anything**; each takes about one second.

```bash
python scripts/run_pipeline.py configs/pipeline_n12.yaml
python scripts/run_pipeline.py configs/pipeline_n20.yaml
```

Or from Python:

```python
from salsa import run_salsa2_pipeline

result = run_salsa2_pipeline("configs/pipeline_n12.yaml")
print(result.exact_recovery, result.verification_passed)
```

Attack setting, with no ground truth consulted at any point:

```bash
python scripts/run_pipeline.py configs/pipeline_n12.yaml --no-evaluate
```

**Outputs.** Each run writes a full `PipelineResult` to the directory named by
`pipeline.output_dir` (default `results/final_pipeline/`), as
`<label>_result.json`. Every experiment is reproducible from its config file plus
its fixed seed; each config records the checkpoint it attacks, and the pipeline
refuses to load a checkpoint whose recorded identity disagrees with the config.

## Testing

```bash
pytest -q
```

**687 passed, 2 skipped.**

The suite covers the mathematics before the neural code — LWE/RLWE generation,
secret sampling, the codec's encode/decode round trip against the original
SALSA encoder — then parameter-count budgets checked three independent ways,
secret-isolation assertions scanned against the source text, the integrated
pipeline end to end, and regression behaviour for every earlier phase. The two
skips are dimension guards, not failures.

## Limitations

Stated plainly, because the numbers above are easy to over-read:

- **Only n=12, h=2 and n=20, h=2 were experimentally demonstrated.**
- **n=30, n=50 and n=128 were never trained or tested.** The architecture accepts
  them and the cost has been estimated, but no model exists at those dimensions.
- **One trained secret and one checkpoint per demonstrated dimension.** Recovery
  was reproduced across three secrets at n=12 (three seeds), but only one at
  n=20. Seed-to-seed variance at n=20 is unmeasured.
- **These are diagnostic low-dimensional instances.** C(12,2) = 66 and
  C(20,2) = 190 possible secrets. Recovering a secret from a 190-element space is
  not an attack on LWE.
- **The results do not establish a practical LWE/RLWE security break**, and
  nothing here should be cited as evidence about deployed parameter sets.
- **Two dimensions is not a scaling law.** Multi-seed dimension scaling remains
  future work.
- The `best_margin` guard has been validated at `sigma = 3` only.

---

## Future work

None of the following has been performed.

- **Larger dimensions** — n=30, n=50, n=128, where the sample requirement, not
  the architecture, is expected to be the binding constraint.
- **Multi-seed validation** — several secrets and seeds per dimension, so
  recovery rates carry an uncertainty estimate rather than a single outcome.
- **Remaining controlled ablations** — variants C, D and E from the component
  attribution design, which would attribute the residual sparse-input margin to
  individual features.
- **Scalability analysis** — measuring how the sample requirement grows with `n`
  and `h`, and whether the one-token representation's advantage holds as the
  sequence lengthens.
